# Signals: how humans actually say things

Flat reference for the **Read** step of `SKILL.md`. Each signal: what it sounds like, what is underneath, what to do. Most messages blend two or three shapes.

## Vibe

*Sounds like:* "make it pop", "clean", "modern", "professional", "make it feel right", "less boring".

*Underneath:* a real dissatisfaction the human cannot name yet. Vague language marks where their words ran out, not where their standards end.

*Do:* translate the vibe into concrete levers for the medium (spacing, hierarchy, contrast, motion, copy tone), pick the two or three most likely to fix the felt problem, and show the result. The result is the question — their reaction to a concrete change teaches you what "pop" meant.

## Trail-off

*Sounds like:* "yk, something like Notion but less... whatever", "idk, just better", trailing ellipses, "or something".

*Underneath:* an image in their head with no word attached at the end. The trail-off is a dig site — the exact spot where intent outran vocabulary.

*Do:* offer completions as defaults, not questions: "I'm reading that as: Notion's structure, minus the setup friction — templates you can use on first open." One candidate, stated, correctable.

## Analogy

*Sounds like:* "like Instagram for podcasts", "Tinder for jobs", "make it feel like a haskel**l** rewrite of that CLI".

*Underneath:* a compressed bundle of borrowed features and one differentiator. Humans reach for analogies because they carry whole design vectors in one word.

*Do:* split it — what is borrowed (the feed mechanic, the swipe mechanic), what is NOT (the thing that makes theirs different — usually the part they were pointing at). State both halves; the "not" half is usually the intent line.

## Pointed finger

*Sounds like:* "fix this", "that thing is broken", "it's ugly", "this again".

*Underneath:* a deictic act. The human is pointing at something in their shared view — the open file, the screenshot, the error on screen, the thing from two turns ago. From their seat the request is complete.

*Do:* resolve every pointer against the environment before asking anything. "This" that cannot be resolved from context is the one case where a question earns its place — name what you can see and ask which they meant.

## Polite ask

*Sounds like:* "could we maybe add auth?", "would it be crazy to...", "I wonder if...", "you couldn't just...?"

*Underneath:* a command in question clothing. Mode-of-speech is weak signal; stakes and context are strong. Mid-project, "could we add X" means "add X".

*Do:* read the goal, weigh reversibility, and act. When the politeness is genuinely a wish for options, the reply's first line ("doing X now — say the word if you wanted a menu instead") keeps it repairable either way.

## Fragment pile

*Sounds like:* stream of consciousness, half a sentence about the signup flow, a jump to the logo, "oh and", no punctuation.

*Underneath:* a brain emptying faster than a keyboard can hold. Each fragment is one ask; the pile is a work order in random order.

*Do:* cluster fragments into asks, order them by what unblocks the rest, state the order, start. Every fragment gets acknowledged — silence on one is how piles come back.

## Wish-list

*Sounds like:* "I want it to do A, B, C... oh and D, E..." with no priorities, no MVP, no sequence.

*Underneath:* the whole dream, delivered at once. The human is showing you the destination, not tonight's step.

*Do:* propose the slice that makes the next slice possible, state it ("starting with A + C — D and E are queued after"), and keep their list visible as the plan so nothing feels dropped.

## Silence gap

*Sounds like:* no audience named, no constraints, no stack mentioned, nothing about where this will run.

*Underneath:* common ground. Everything omitted is, from their seat, already obvious — sitting in the repo, the framework on disk, the conversation's earlier turns, or the industry they assume you know.

*Do:* fill gaps from the environment, switch the chosen defaults on, and name them. A stated default is steerable; a silent one is a trap you set for yourself.

## Frustration

*Sounds like:* "this STILL doesn't work", "third time", "ugh", sarcasm.

*Underneath:* a history — two or three attempts already failed. The strongest signal is not the words; it is that the previous interpretations missed, so the intent line needs rebuilding from the failure pattern, not the latest sentence.

*Do:* find what the previous attempts had in common, treat that shared assumption as the suspect, change something structural, and say what you changed and why. Match their seriousness; keep it short.

## Excitement

*Sounds like:* "wait this is amazing, what if it ALSO...", rapid-fire ideas, superlatives.

*Underneath:* scope expanding in real time. Energy is real, and so is the risk of building five half-things.

*Do:* ride the energy, pick the one idea that compounds, queue the rest visibly. Enthusiasm plus a stated sequence keeps momentum and prevents the wreckage.
